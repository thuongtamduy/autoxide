Forms

Tiếng Việt: FORMS.vi.md

The forms module keeps form definitions, takes submissions from the public (contact forms, sign-ups for a consultation, simple surveys), checks every answer on the server, keeps them in an admin inbox, mails the people in charge, and exports or erases them. It is API only: the website shows the form and sends it; the admin screens are a separate project. It lives under /api/v1/forms. The full contract is in openapi.json.

No third-party account is needed: spam is stopped by the core’s shared anti-spam layer (a hidden trap field, a signed form token, rate limits, duplicate detection; an optional self-hosted proof-of-work challenge).

Permissions

PermissionAllows
forms.readReading forms and their submissions (personal data)
forms.manageCreating, changing, opening, closing and deleting forms
forms.reviewMarking submissions read, archived, spam or not spam; deleting one
forms.exportCSV export; exporting and erasing every submission of an email address
forms.submitSending submissions without the anti-spam checks for anonymous visitors (a website server’s own API key)

People who fill in a form need none: everything under /api/v1/forms/public/ is open.

  • Setting or changing notify_emails needs forms.read as well as forms.manage: the notification mails carry the answers, so someone who may not read submissions cannot send them to their own inbox.
  • Give forms.submit only to the API key of a website server; being signed in is not enough to skip the anti-spam checks (see The website).

Stores and sellers

With an organization tree (module organizations), a form may belong to a unit: org_id when creating it; omitted, it is a form of the whole tenant. forms.read, forms.manage, forms.review and forms.export follow the scope of the role: a store’s staff see and manage only their store’s forms and submissions, and forms of the whole tenant need a tenant-wide scope. Exporting or deleting every submission of an email touches every form, so it needs forms.export for the whole tenant. Filter the list with owner_org.

Define a form

curl -X POST http://localhost:8080/api/v1/forms \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: $(uuidgen)" \
  -d '{"key": "contact", "name": "Contact us",
       "fields": [
         {"key": "name", "type": "text", "label": {"en": "Name", "vi": "Tên"}, "required": true},
         {"key": "email", "type": "email", "label": {"en": "Email"}, "required": true},
         {"key": "message", "type": "textarea", "label": {"en": "Message", "vi": "Lời nhắn"}},
         {"key": "agree", "type": "consent", "required": true,
          "label": {"en": "You may contact me about my request.", "vi": "Tôi đồng ý được liên hệ."}}
       ],
       "allowed_origins": ["https://www.example.com"],
       "notify_emails": ["sales@example.com"],
       "success_message": {"en": "Thank you!", "vi": "Cảm ơn bạn!"}}'
  • Field types (closed list): text, email, phone, textarea, number (min, max), select, radio, checkbox (several values), date (YYYY-MM-DD), hidden (filled by the page, e.g. a campaign tag), consent (a box that must not be ticked in advance; its label is the text shown). text, textarea and hidden take min_length / max_length. Labels, help texts, placeholders and option labels are {"en": …, "vi": …}.
  • Every problem of a definition is reported at once (fields[2].options…).
  • Versions. Changing fields makes a new version. Each submission keeps the version it was sent with and is always shown with that version’s labels (answers[], and for choice fields the chosen options with their labels in answers[].choices), so old submissions stay readable after the form changes.
  • PATCH and DELETE of a form need If-Match (the ETag you read). Closing a form ({"status": "closed"}) keeps its submissions; deleting it deletes them too.
  • notify_emails are mailed for each new submission, with Reply-To set to the sender’s address (left out if the mail service cannot use that address). They are never shown by the public endpoints, and recipients never come from what a visitor sends. Setting them needs forms.read too.
  • Email answers are checked the way the mail service counts them: at most 64 bytes before the @ and 254 bytes in all, measured in UTF-8 after lower-casing (é takes two bytes).

The website

  1. GET /api/v1/forms/public/contact?locale=vi — the fields with texts in one language, the token_scope and the success message. No admin settings are included.
  2. Each time the form is shown (never in cached HTML): GET /api/v1/antispam/token?scope=forms:contact — a form token. Show a hidden trap input that people leave empty.
  3. POST /api/v1/forms/public/contact/submissions:
{"data": {"name": "Ada", "email": "ada@example.com", "message": "Hello", "agree": true},
 "locale": "vi", "page_url": "https://www.example.com/contact",
 "form_token": "…", "trap": ""}
  • The answer is 201 {"data": {"id": …, "message": "Cảm ơn bạn!"}} — the same whether the submission was accepted, held as spam (sent too fast, duplicate) or dropped (trap filled, too many from one network or address): bots learn nothing.
  • Wrong answers: 422 with every problem in errors (data.email → email, unknown keys → unknown; past 50 unknown keys, the rest are counted in one data entry). A missing or expired token: 400 antispam.token_* — reload the form. A closed form: 409 forms.closed. A page whose Origin is not in allowed_origins: 403 forms.origin_not_allowed (an empty list allows any site).
  • Sending the same form_token again (double click, flaky network) returns the first id and stores nothing new.
  • A website server may send the form with its own API key instead, if that key has the permission forms.submit: no token needed, limited to AUTOXIDE_FORMS_SUBMISSIONS_PER_USER_PER_HOUR; use an Idempotency-Key header. Any other signed-in caller (for example a self-registered account) goes through the same checks as an anonymous visitor: without a form token it gets 400 antispam.token_missing.

The inbox

  • GET /api/v1/forms and GET /api/v1/forms/{id} give each form’s submissions: how many are new, read, archived and spam.
  • GET /api/v1/forms/{id}/submissions?status=&q= — newest first, spam left out unless asked; q searches the answers ignoring case and accents.
  • PATCH /api/v1/forms/submissions/{id} {"status": "read" | "archived" | "spam" | "new"} with If-Match. Moving a submission out of spam mails the people in charge as for a new one.
  • GET /api/v1/forms/{id}/submissions/export?format=csv — CSV for spreadsheets. Cells that start with =, +, -, @, tab or carriage return get a leading ' so they are never run as formulas.

Large exports

For a form with many submissions, export in the background instead of holding a request open:

  1. POST /api/v1/forms/{id}/submissions/exports (body optional: {"status": "new"} like the status of the export above) answers 201 at once, with the export’s address in Location. A form has one export running at a time; asking again meanwhile answers 409 forms.export_in_progress.
  2. GET /api/v1/forms/exports/{export_id} gives state: pending, running, then ready (or failed: ask for a new one). Once ready, download_url is a link to the file — the same content as the export above, byte for byte.
  3. The link needs no sign-in, so it can be opened in a browser; it works for AUTOXIDE_FORMS_EXPORT_LINK_MINUTES (default 60). Do not share it. Ask for the export again for a fresh link. It is an absolute URL when AUTOXIDE_HTTP_PUBLIC_URL is set.

Both need forms.export. The file is written to the shared file storage (module files, local disk or S3) under forms/exports/ and deleted after AUTOXIDE_FORMS_EXPORT_KEEP_HOURS (default 24) by the daily cleanup job.

Personal data

  • GET /api/v1/forms/data?email=… lists, and DELETE /api/v1/forms/data?email=… deletes for good, every submission of an address, in every form (permission forms.export) — for requests of the person the data is about. A submission counts when the address is its sender’s email, or when any answer is exactly that address (ignoring case), such as a second email field (“your friend’s email”). An address only mentioned inside a longer answer (a message) is not found: search the inbox with q for those.
  • Network addresses are never stored: only a keyed hash of the network block, cleared after AUTOXIDE_FORMS_IP_RETENTION_DAYS.
  • A ticked consent box stores the exact text shown, the form version, the time and the source.
  • Events (and so webhooks) carry ids and the form key only, never answers or addresses; other systems read details through the API with a permission.
  • When an account is deleted, forms it created stay; created_by becomes null.
  • Deleting a form deletes its submissions. A submission whose form no longer exists is deleted by the daily cleanup job.
  • Large exports hold personal data. Deleting the submissions of an address, or deleting a form, also deletes every export file of the forms concerned. Export files left are deleted after AUTOXIDE_FORMS_EXPORT_KEEP_HOURS. Backups of the file storage copy forms/exports/ too while the files exist. The event forms.submissions_exported records who exported which form (for the audit log), without the file.

Legal

Retention times are settings, not rules written in the code. Whether a contact form needs consent, how long submissions may be kept, and how fast data requests must be answered depend on the law that applies to you (for example Vietnam’s Personal Data Protection Law of 2025 and Decree 356/2025): check the original texts for your case and set AUTOXIDE_FORMS_RETENTION_DAYS (or the tenant’s forms.retention_days, or each form’s retention_days) accordingly.

AI agents (MCP)

Tools, with the same permissions and checks as REST:

  • forms.list, forms.get (forms.read); forms.create and forms.update (forms.manage; setting notify_emails also needs forms.read). forms.get returns the form with an etag; pass it back as etag to forms.update, like If-Match (it is not the form’s version, which counts changes to the fields). Close a form with forms.update and status: closed.
  • forms.list_submissions, forms.get_submission (forms.read) and forms.set_submission_status (forms.review; pass the submission’s version you read).

There is no tool to delete a form (that also deletes its submissions, and cannot be undone; use REST) and no tool to send a form.

A form may set its own ip_limit_per_hour, email_limit_per_hour and retention_days. The anti-spam layer itself (AUTOXIDE_ANTISPAM_*) is shared by every module. All are described in .env.example.

With several tenants, a tenant can replace AUTOXIDE_FORMS_RETENTION_DAYS with its own forms.retention_days (0 to 36500; its administrator with PUT /api/v1/kernel/settings/forms.retention_days, permission kernel.settings.manage; platform staff with PUT /api/v1/kernel/tenants/{id}/settings/forms.retention_days). The order is: the form’s retention_days, then the tenant’s setting, then the server’s. A wrong value is refused when it is set; DELETE the setting to go back to the server’s value.
