Plans, subscriptions and limits

Tiếng Việt

The plans module lets you sell Autoxide by plan without changing code. A plan is data: which modules a tenant may use, which features are on, how much it may use (limits), and its prices. Tenants get a plan through a subscription; the kernel enforces it on every request, MCP tool, job and event.

Nothing changes until you create a plan: a tenant with no subscription gets the default plan, and with no default plan it is not limited at all. The platform tenant (the operator) is never limited.

Everything below is done by platform staff — users of the platform tenant holding plans.plans.manage / plans.subscriptions.manage — over REST (/api/v1/plans/...) or MCP (plans.* tools).

Create a plan

See which modules, features and limits exist in GET /api/v1/kernel/modules (features, limits, always_enabled, requires). Modules marked always_enabled (sign-in, files, audit…) are on for everyone.

curl -X POST https://api.example.com/api/v1/plans/plans \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"code": "pro", "name": "Pro", "status": "active", "trial_days": 14,
       "modules": ["content", "media", "forms"],
       "features": ["content.ai_suggestions", "auth.api_keys"],
       "limits": {"organizations.units": 10, "auth.members": 25,
                  "files.storage_bytes": 10737418240, "kernel.api_requests": null}}'
  • A limit set to null is unlimited; a limit left out is 0.
  • A limit that has parts (parts in GET /api/v1/kernel/modules) also takes a cap per part: "organizations.units.store": 2 allows at most 2 units of type store, within the overall organizations.units. A part left out has no cap of its own.
  • A base plan that turns on a module must turn on the modules it needs (requires), or the request answers plans.module_dependency_missing.
  • "is_default": true makes it the plan of tenants without a subscription (only one plan is the default).
  • "kind": "addon" makes an add-on: its limits are added on top of the base plan, times the quantity bought.
  • Lifecycle settings per plan: grace_days (7), suspend_days (30), retention_days (90), renewal_lead_days (7).

Add a price with POST /api/v1/plans/plans/{id}/prices ({"kind": "recurring", "interval": "month", "amount": {"amount": "199000", "currency": "VND"}}). Prices never change: add a new one and deactivate the old one (POST /prices/{id}/deactivate); subscriptions keep their price until they change plan. A plan is never deleted, only retired (POST /plans/{id}/retire).

Create a tenant on a plan

Platform staff with plans.provision create a tenant ready to use in one call: the tenant, its subscription, the sample data of the modules the plan turns on (when the plan or the request asks), and an email invitation of its administrator with the role admin.

curl -X POST https://api.example.com/api/v1/plans/provisionings \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"slug": "acme", "name": "Acme Ltd", "plan_id": "...", "admin_email": "owner@acme.example"}'

It runs in the background: follow it with GET /api/v1/plans/provisionings/{id} (steps, and error when it failed); fix the cause, then POST …/retry goes on from the last step done. From the server: autoxide tenant create --slug acme --name "Acme Ltd" --plan <plan id> --admin-email owner@acme.example does the same at once. A dedicated plan needs placement: a placement of kind dedicated added first with autoxide placement add. A plan that requires_kyc cannot be provisioned by staff: the tenant signs up by itself and passes the identity checks (next section).

Tenants sign up by themselves

Turn it on with AUTOXIDE_PLANS_SIGNUP=true and mark the plans people may pick with "self_signup": true (active base plans). The sign-up page of your website talks to the API on the platform host (or sends Autoxide-Tenant: <platform slug>), without signing in:

  1. GET /api/v1/antispam/token?scope=plans:signup for a form token, then POST /api/v1/plans/signups with email, tenant_name, slug, plan_code, "accept_terms": true (the person accepts the platform’s current terms of use and privacy policy) and the form_token. The answer is always 202; invalid values answer 422 with every problem at once (plan_code not open to sign-up, slug taken…).
  2. A mail goes to the address with a link valid 24 hours (AUTOXIDE_PLANS_SIGNUP_URL, with {token}; without it the mail carries the token). The page sends the token to POST /api/v1/plans/signups/confirm: the acceptance of the terms is recorded (module compliance), and
    • for a plan that requires_kyc, the sign-up becomes awaiting_kyc and kyc.url is where the person passes the identity checks (calling confirm again starts new checks; after them the provider sends the person to AUTOXIDE_PLANS_SIGNUP_RETURN_URL). Passed → the tenant is created; failed → rejected (kyc_rejected);
    • otherwise the tenant is created at once.
  3. Creating the tenant is the same provisioning staff run (provisioning_id); the person is invited by mail as the administrator (role admin). Follow it with GET /api/v1/plans/signups/{id}?token=…: pending_email → (awaiting_kyc) → provisioning → completed (tenant_id), or rejected (reason) / expired.

Sending the same email and slug again while waiting sends a new mail (the old link stops working). Two people waiting for the same slug: the first to reach the provisioning keeps it, the other is rejected (tenant_slug_taken). Unconfirmed sign-ups expire after 24 hours, unfinished identity checks after 30 days; ended sign-ups are deleted 30 days later. Staff list them with GET /api/v1/plans/signups (plans.subscriptions.read, MCP plans.list_signups).

Put a tenant on a plan

curl -X PUT https://api.example.com/api/v1/plans/tenants/$TENANT/subscription \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"plan_id": "...", "price_id": "...", "addons": [{"plan_id": "...", "quantity": 2}]}'

The first time this starts the plan’s trial (unless "trial": false, or the tenant already had one). For one tenant you can also turn a module or feature on or off, or replace a limit, with a reason and an optional end: POST /api/v1/plans/tenants/{id}/overrides.

Changes take effect at once on the server that made them and within AUTOXIDE_PLANS_CACHE_SECONDS (default 30) on the others.

What tenants see

  • GET /api/v1/plans/entitlements (any signed-in user): modules, features, each limit with its use, and access (full or read_only) — user interfaces hide what the plan does not include.
  • An endpoint of a module the plan does not include answers 403 module_not_enabled; a feature 403 feature_not_enabled; a full limit 403 limit_exceeded; the monthly request quota 429 quota_exhausted (only when the plan sets a maximum).
  • With plans.billing.read / plans.billing.manage: GET /subscription, GET /usage, GET /billing-periods, POST /subscription/change (an upgrade applies at once and bills the rest of the period; a downgrade applies when the period ends), POST /subscription/cancel, POST /subscription/resume.

Lifecycle

StatusAccessNext
trialingfulltrial ends: free plan → active; priced → paid next period → active, else past_due

past_duefull (grace)paid → active; grace_days pass → suspended
suspendedread-onlypaid → active; suspend_days pass → canceled
canceledread-onlyresumed before delete_after → active; retention_days pass → deleted
deletednonethe tenant and its data are deleted for good (accounting records modules must keep stay)

A job moves subscriptions every 5 minutes. renewal_lead_days before a period ends, the next billing period is opened and a reminder is mailed to the subscription’s billing email (AUTOXIDE_PLANS_REMINDER_DAYS, default 7,1), again when overdue and every 3 days of the grace period.

Payments (manual for now)

Until a payment provider is connected (step 36), billing periods are paid outside the system: check the bank transfer, then POST /api/v1/plans/billing-periods/{id}/mark-paid ({"provider_reference": "..."}); a past-due or suspended subscription becomes active again. POST /billing-periods/{id}/void cancels a period opened by mistake.

Platform staff see a tenant’s billing periods with GET /api/v1/plans/tenants/{id}/billing-periods and its billing profile (legal name, tax code, invoice email, address — the tenant sets it with PUT /api/v1/plans/billing-profile) with GET /api/v1/plans/tenants/{id}/billing-profile.

To block a tenant completely (reads too), use POST /api/v1/plans/tenants/{id}/subscription/suspend with a reason; undo it with PATCH /api/v1/kernel/tenants/{id} ("status": "active"). GET /api/v1/kernel/tenants?subscription_status=past_due and ?near_limit=true list tenants to follow up.

Exporting a tenant’s data

Platform staff (kernel.tenants.operate) and administrators of the tenant itself (kernel.data.export) can export all of a tenant’s data as one JSON Lines file: POST /api/v1/kernel/tenants/{id}/exports, then GET …/exports/{export_id} until it is ready, then GET …/exports/{export_id}/content (or POST …/link for a 24-hour link that works without signing in). The first line describes the export; every other line is {"table": "...", "row": {...}}. Password hashes and secrets are left out, and so are the contents of uploaded files (their records are included; download them through the files API). When a subscription is canceled an export is started automatically. Exports are deleted after AUTOXIDE_TENANCY_EXPORT_RETENTION_DAYS (30).

Deleting a tenant

POST /api/v1/kernel/tenants/{id}/erase (kernel.tenants.delete, with {"confirm": "<slug>", "reason": "..."}) deletes a suspended tenant and its data for good; it cannot be undone. A subscription that passes its retention period does this by itself.
